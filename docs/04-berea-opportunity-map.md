# Berea opportunity map

The supplied matrix identifies five small-business categories in Berea. This is an initial prospecting hypothesis, not proof of demand. Public contact details remain in the source CSV; outreach should begin with discovery, not a product pitch.

## Opportunity sequence

| Priority | Category | Proposed wedge | Why it fits UofC | Primary caution |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Food trucks and pop-ups | owner sends one update that synchronizes location, hours, menu, and sold-out items | narrow workflow, obvious user, visible outcome, reusable across vendors | platform integrations and alert consent |
| 2 | Artisans and crafters | photo-to-draft inventory listing | highly demonstrable, bounded, human approval before publishing | measurements and descriptions must be verified |
| 3 | Auto and trade services | structured photo/video intake and quote preparation | strong time-saving potential and reusable intake pattern | avoid presenting an AI output as a binding estimate |
| 4 | Dining and hospitality | after-hours FAQ and reservation assistant | clear call-volume pain and measurable deflection | allergens, booking accuracy, and voice reliability |
| 5 | Farm-to-table and grocers | prep and waste recommendation | meaningful upside and potential vertical product | needs sufficient clean sales, weather, and event data |

## Recommended first pilot

Start with **location and menu synchronization for a food truck or pop-up**.

The first version should not attempt autonomous marketing. It should:

1. accept a simple structured update from the owner;
2. show a preview of every downstream message;
3. require approval;
4. update one or two channels;
5. retain a time-stamped activity log;
6. measure update time and customer confusion before and after.

This creates a reusable “single update → many channels” module that can later serve event vendors, restaurants, and service businesses.

## Discovery questions for every category

- Walk us through the last time this problem happened.
- How do you handle it now?
- Who enters the information, who consumes it, and who approves it?
- How often does it happen?
- What does the delay or error cost in time, waste, missed revenue, or trust?
- Which part requires human judgment?
- What information is sensitive?
- What would make you refuse to use a new tool?
- What result would make a 30-day pilot worthwhile?

## Qualification rubric

Score each prospect from 0–2 on:

- problem frequency;
- value of improvement;
- access to the actual user;
- clarity of the workflow;
- availability of safe test data;
- ability to test within two weeks;
- repeatability across similar businesses;
- manageable production risk.

Prefer pilots with a high total and no zero in **access to the actual user**, **safe test data**, or **manageable production risk**.

## What not to promise

- fully autonomous posting, quoting, booking, or purchasing;
- guaranteed forecasts from limited data;
- replacement of professional judgment;
- production access before review;
- a custom feature before the underlying workflow is understood.
