# “Home Schooled” podcast

## Concept

**Home Schooled** is a Thoughtful Technology podcast about connecting the dots and finding your way home.

It asks how the home a person grew up in shaped the home they are building now—and the knowledge, values, and heritage they hope to pass forward. Technology is present, but the subject is the person behind the work.

## Editorial promise

> Do not walk in and give us your resume.

The show looks for what a biography or job title leaves out:

- the leap of faith;
- the rebellion against a default path;
- the people who formed the guest’s taste;
- what the guest values more than status;
- what “home” means after moving across places, systems, or identities;
- what the guest would pass to a former self, a future self, or their children.

## Audience

People reconsidering a default path:

- high school students deciding whether and where to attend college;
- college students deciding how to work;
- people considering a career change;
- builders, parents, and community members looking for a different frame.

## Episode format

Target length: **20–30 minutes**

### Part I — The person (10–15 minutes)

Begin somewhere no resume begins:

1. What is your fun fact?
2. Where did you grow up, and what did “home” mean there?
3. What is not on your resume that explains who you are?
4. Which people made you who you are?
5. Were your defining qualities present early, or made over time?
6. When did you first become uncommon among your peer group?

### Part II — The turn (5–10 minutes)

Find the decision or rupture:

1. When was your leap of faith?
2. Was there a moment you rebelled against “the system”?
3. What did you stop optimizing for?
4. When did your time become more valuable than the path you were on?
5. What would you say to the version of yourself at that point?

### Part III — The takeaway (10–15 minutes)

Give the listener something to carry home:

1. How do you cultivate taste?
2. Do you believe we become the average of the people around us?
3. How should someone build the community they need?
4. What are you building now, beyond your title?
5. If you have children—or people you mentor—what do you hope to pass on?
6. What question are you still trying to answer?

## Recurring elements

- **The object from home:** the guest brings or shows one object that carries a story.
- **Former self / future self:** one message backward and one promise forward.
- **Choose your own adventure:** the guest names the path they declined and what became possible.
- **The open chair:** a person the guest believes the audience should learn from next.

## Visual language

The setting should feel cozy, intimate, and inhabited: warm practical light, natural materials, a dining-room-table posture, and a sense that the guest has welcomed the listener into a real world.

There is an unresolved conflict in the source notes:

- one direction requires a consistent Google Meet background in the church or Longtail building;
- another requires each guest to be seated in their own home with their actual home as the background.

Recommended resolution: make **the guest’s home** the editorial rule because it best embodies the premise, while using the church or Longtail setting for host introductions, local guests, and consistent promotional footage. If production consistency is the overriding need, choose the fixed set and describe it as the show’s shared dining room. Do not attempt to claim both as a non-negotiable.

## Relationship to UofC

The podcast should not become a long commercial. Its value to UofC comes from widening the community:

- students hear credible alternatives to conventional paths;
- guests can become mentors, clients, introducers, or supporters;
- the show models the same curiosity used in client discovery;
- each episode contributes knowledge to the “home” the incubator is building.

A restrained closing line is enough:

> Home Schooled is made by the University of College, where young people learn to build thoughtful technology with real communities.

## Pilot episode test

Record three episodes with people already inside the relationship network:

1. one person who left a traditional path;
2. one local builder or business owner;
3. one parent, educator, or mentor focused on heritage.

After each recording, test whether a listener can answer:

- What made this person human rather than impressive?
- What was the real turning point?
- What can I do or ask differently this week?
- Did the setting feel like being welcomed into a home?
